I used Claude Code to help me write the functions in this assignment and analyze the trajectories.

For function writing, my process is as follows:
1. I look at the function, understand the code, and understand what is conceptually asked of me.
2. I describe how I would implement the function, the design choices I'd make, and any constraints I'd highlight. I ask Claude for its thoughts and suggestions, and I iterate on it until I am confident in the design.
3. I ask Claude to implement the function based on our design. 

I did this to optimize my time-to-learning ratio -- I believe the concepts taught in this course and assignment are very important, so those parts I make sure to understand myself. Actual implementation details are relatively more boilerplate; I trust Claude to produce correct and idiomatic Python, and having it handle those details allows me to focus on the higher-level concepts.

For trajectory analysis, my process is as follows:
1. After a run is produced, I located the trajectory file submitted.
2. I take a prelimiary look to understand the format and outputs of the trajectory file. 
3. I come up with a list of questions I want to answer, and blueprints on how to answer them. I then ask Claude to analyze the trajectories using scripts based on those blueprints.
4. Based on my own knowledge and observations, I make modifications or ask Claude to make modifications to the analysis to improve its clarity and accuracy. 
5. For the written documents, I select what I believe to be the relevant analyses produced from previous steps and ask Claude to reproduce them into the .md file. 

This approach I believe mixes my own understanding but leaves the majority of the boilerplate to Claude, optimizing learning and time.
